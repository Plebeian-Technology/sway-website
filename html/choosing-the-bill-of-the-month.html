Choosing the Sway Bill of the Month

Choosing a Bill of the Month can be a difficult process. Sometimes the choice is obvious: an issue gains momentum, inspires public debate, and the legislature writes and votes on a bill. That does not happen often. Most months Sway relies on an imperfect process to decide which bill to feature.

Who chooses

Sway administrators choose each Bill of the Month by hand. An administrator schedules one bill for each release day, and a day holds only one bill. On that day the bill appears as the Bill of the Month and Sway notifies users who opted in.

How candidates are ranked

The tool administrators use to pick a bill lists candidates in order of an interest score. The score measures how much disagreement a bill carries. It weighs five signals:

  • How many legislators voted against their own party.
  • What share of the chamber abstained.
  • How far the positions of organizations on the bill are split.
  • How actively citizens submitted hearing testimony.
  • How recently the bill moved.

Some jurisdictions do not publish how each member voted. Baltimore City is one. For those places the score uses only the split among organizations and how recently the bill moved. The score orders the list. It does not choose the bill.

What editors weigh

When choosing a Bill of the Month, Sway considers a few factors:

  1. Diversity. The Sway platform should offer users a variety of bills to vote on. For example, police reform is a popular topic in Baltimore City, and Sway will feature legislation on reforming the Baltimore City Police Department. However, police reform cannot be the only issue Sway covers, and it would be rare for a specific category of legislation to be Bill of the Month two months in a row.
  2. Cost. Sway avoids legislation that results in large spending increases, such as Bill 20-0588 from the 2020 Baltimore City Council, which approved a $105 million bond issuance for the redevelopment of the Perkins-Somerset-Oldtown area of East Baltimore. An exception to this rule is regular, broad spending bills such as the annual budget.
  3. Focused. Sway only considers legislation that targets an issue. A broad budget bill is not targeted in the way a bill that spends money on low-income housing is focused on the specific issue of housing.
  4. Impactful. This is the final criterion. The Baltimore City Council considers many bills that may matter in a hyperlocal setting, such as the demolition of some row homes. Such an ordinance would have little impact on Baltimore City as a whole.

When several bills deserve the title

Even with these criteria, choosing can be no easy task. Some people will be disappointed in a month when several bills deserve the title. Sway can ease this by selecting bills that are past their peak popularity. A bill not selected one month may be selected the next, or during a slow period such as around July 4th or the holidays at the end of the year.

Sway may also re-issue a bill as the Bill of the Month. This is most likely during those slow periods, when the legislature has not voted on any new legislation.
